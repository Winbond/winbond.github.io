Mobile manipulator for warehouse assistance In progress

Jul 2026 to now

Independent project. Mechanical, electrical, firmware and ROS 2, all mine.

A mecanum-wheel base carrying a 5-DoF arm, built to pick and place items in a warehouse. A Jetson Orin Nano runs perception and ROS 2 planning; an STM32 handles real-time motor and servo control, and the two talk over UART. I started it to learn the software layers I couldn't contribute to on my capstone.

What I've done so far

  • Modeled the chassis in Onshape and 3D-printed it.
  • Chose the hardware: 4 Hall-encoder gear motors, 5 HX-12H joint servos with an HTS-21H gripper, an Aurora 930 Pro depth camera and a Coin-D6 LiDAR.
  • Designed a power distribution PCB for an 11.1 V 3S pack, with buck regulation to 6 V, 9 V and 7 V.
  • Teaching myself ROS 2 and YOLO to bring up teleoperation and depth-fused object detection.

Assistive 7-DoF robotic arm for daily living

Sep 2025 to Jun 2026

Electrical Lead, senior capstone (ECE 129), team of 4

A 7-DoF arm that helps people with injured or limited hand function get through everyday tasks. The arm scans the table with its camera, works out what is there, and offers choices that the user picks with an ESP32 Bluetooth joystick.

In the kitchen, it reads which seasonings are on the counter and lists the recipes it can make. Once the user picks a dish, it cooks step by step: picks up the spatula and stirs, sets it down, grabs a seasoning jar, adds it, puts the jar back and keeps going until the dish is done. For everyday help, it lists the objects it sees, such as a phone, a straw or a pen, and hands over the one the user selects.

The hardware: a 250 g-payload arm with servo control on an STM32 Nucleo F446RE and AprilTag vision on a Jetson Orin Nano. I owned the electrical subsystem and the low-level joint control. The perception and task-planning software didn't reach a working end-to-end demo by the end of the capstone. That gap is why I'm now learning ROS 2 and YOLO on my own robot.

What I did

  • Architected a multi-rail power system from a 19.5 V / 240 W input: four 7.0 V buck branches sharing the servo load, dedicated 11.5 V and 8.75 V rails, plus fuse and TVS protection.
  • Designed custom PCBs (buck converters, power distribution, Nucleo I/O shield, controller BMS) through 3 faculty-reviewed iterations.
  • Wrote joint control in C++ that turns kinematic angle targets into calibrated servo commands.

Hardest problemNoise on the converter outputs. I tightened the feedback loop into a compact, ground-referenced layout and widened the copper power pours, then checked every rail on the oscilloscope under load.

4-DoF teleoperated arm with IMU and flex sensors

Jan to Mar 2026

Embedded Lead, ECE 167 Sensing & Sensor Technologies final project

A wearable controller that makes a robotic arm copy your hand. IMU orientation and flex/force readings map 1:1 to servo commands, so the arm mimics the operator's motion.

What I did

  • Wrote interrupt-driven C firmware on an STM32F411RE reading the IMU, flex/FSR, encoder and ultrasonic sensors over I²C, ADC, PWM and interrupts.
  • Calibrated the IMU with bias/scale correction and ellipsoid fitting, then cleaned the signals with IIR filtering and regression.
  • Co-designed dual-buck power distribution with per-servo decoupling.

Hardest problemServo jitter. I reworked the control loop with a deadband, low-pass filtering and rate limiting until the arm held still when my hand did.

Autonomous package-sorting conveyor

Apr to Jun 2025

Mechanical Lead, ECE 118 Mechatronics final project

A conveyor that sorts packages into 6 bins by color and weight, using a load cell, a color sensor, IR beam-breaks and servo pushers, all run by hierarchical state machines in C on a PIC32.

What I did

  • Designed every mechanical part in Onshape: belt chassis, load-cell weighing station, servo pusher arm, swappable sorting slots, and a roller mount with adjustable belt tension.
  • Fabricated all of it with FDM 3D printing.
  • Added sensor-gated flow control: the next package is weighed and released only after the beam-breaks confirm the belt is clear.

Hardest problemPackages were landing in the wrong weight class depending on how they were placed. Dropped light ones read heavy, and slowly released heavy ones read light. A settling delay before sampling the load cell fixed it.

Measured

100 %
sorting accuracy across 6 categories
15–18 / min
throughput, 2.5–3× the required 6 per minute

Why I build robots

My parents work in the auto industry, and I grew up surrounded by the model cars my father brought home.

  1. A Mini 4WD that couldn't see the track

    As a kid I raced Mini 4WD cars, measuring parts with calipers and tuning damping and center of gravity. However well I tuned them, they had no idea where the track went.

  2. A capstone arm that stalled on software

    On my capstone I built the power system and low-level control. When the vision and planning stack stalled, I didn't yet know ROS 2 or perception well enough to help fix it.

  3. A robot that sees and listens

    So I'm building one where I write every layer, from the power board to object detection. The long-term goal is a home robot that takes chores off a family's hands.
